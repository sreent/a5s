# Starting Values

**Status:** Draft v0.3 · **Under:** [Architecture](architecture.md)

These are the numbers a5s starts from, by the phase that needs them, and where each is set: in the signed settings, which its owner approves; in the deployment's [configuration](configuration.md); in an agent's definition; or in the code, which only a release changes, and which the release's approvers sign. The code marks each it holds as a starting value. They're starting values, not decisions: each is baselined in the first months and then set, and loosening one later needs someone outside the team ([independence](architecture.md#independence)). People hold [roles](agent-model.md#roles) as each deployment assigns them, and a scenario can bring numbers of its own, such as a limit on how many records one piece of work may change.

## Phase 1

| Setting | Starting value | Why | Owned by | Where it's set |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Context per run | 24,000 tokens for the assistant and 16,000 for the watcher, with a budget for each part of the context, tested at release. A topic's summary takes a third of it at most, and keeps its newest 500 facts. | Each model call carries the whole context, so it sets what every call costs | The agent owner | Each agent's definition (`context`) |
| Database connections | Each part keeps up to 10 (`A5S_DB_POOL_SIZE`), and a transaction that can't have one within half a second connects afresh | Connecting for each transaction took some 28 connections for each message in the soak test, and pooling takes 2, one for each model call, with answers a quarter faster. Against a managed Postgres each connection is a TLS handshake and a new server process | The platform owner | The deployment (`A5S_DB_POOL_SIZE`) |
| Settings cache | None: the settings in force are checked at every call | A withdrawn approval takes effect at the next call, and settings that stop checking out stop every call | The platform owner |
| Settings requests | 5 a day per person in the studio's settings, with one waiting at a time, and 64 KB at most | Approvers aren't asked again and again | The platform owner |
| Ledger anchor | Every hour, with assurance | A rewritten history shows within the hour | Assurance | The code, by a release |
| Digest | One a day per person, by their own date, from all the agents together, at a time they choose, 09:00 by default. It waits 30 minutes at most for its agents' work. | One place to look, at a time that suits them, and never held all day by work that can't be done | Each person | Each person, for its time; the code, for the rest |
| Deliveries | 5 tries for a chat, after 1, 2, 4 and 8 minutes, then the person's local inbox | A chat that's down for a while loses nothing | The platform owner | The code, by a release |
| Urgent messages | Up to 5 per person per day across all agents, then batched hourly in their working hours. Reminders people asked for at a particular time don't count. | Urgent stays urgent without becoming noise | The platform owner | The code, by a release |
| Chases | Routine items in the last digest before half the turnaround has passed, then escalated when due, but no sooner than a working day after the chase. Urgent items by urgent message at half the turnaround, then escalated when due. | People get a chance to act before anyone above them hears | Each part owner | The code, by a release |
| Working hours | From the directory, and otherwise 09:00 to 17:00, Monday to Friday, in each person's time zone. Hours that end before they start cross midnight, as a night shift's do. Chases and escalations wait for them. | Nobody is chased off shift | Each person, through the directory | The directory |
| Missed schedules | A time more than an hour past is let go, and one that falls while the instance is suspended isn't made up | No backlog of stale work after an outage or a suspension | The agent owner | The code, by a release |
| Conversations | Kept for 12 months after the last message, and archived when someone leaves | Covers a yearly cycle | The data protection officer | The signed settings (`data_protection.kept_for`) |
| Someone who leaves | What a5s keeps for them is archived where it is, a day after the directory stops naming them, and goes on the same clock. Nothing is copied elsewhere | A day lets a directory that missed someone for a while catch up; anything longer leaves their conversation to anyone given the same id | The data protection officer | The code, by a release |
| What people owed | Kept for 12 months after the records stopped owing it | The measures look back a month or two, and a record that opens again is owed afresh | The data protection officer | The signed settings (`data_protection.kept_for`) |
| A person's use of a day's budget, and the log of their digests | Their last two days, and nothing once they leave | Only their day reads either | The data protection officer | The code, by a release |
| Ledger | As long as the records it supports, which the scenario sets, with its copies locked a year at least | It's evidence, and a copy locked for less could be gone before anyone looks | Assurance | The deployment, only longer (`A5S_ANCHOR_RETAIN_DAYS`); a release, shorter |
| Traces | 14 days | For debugging only | The agent owner | Where the deployment sends them, which keeps them |
| Budgets | Set from current volumes, with 20% kept for obligations, a warning at 80%, and routine work waiting at 100%. A release's budgets must fit 6 model calls a run and 12 answers a day, of its whole context and 6,000 tokens beside it. The assistant starts at 200,000 tokens a run and 1,000,000 a person a day, the watcher at 150,000 and 700,000. | Not from a quiet first month, and never so tight that ordinary work can't fit | The platform owner | Each agent's definition (`budget`) |
| Circuit breaker | Opens after 3 failures in a row, and retries every 5 minutes | Stops quickly, and recovers without anyone stepping in | The agent owner | The code, by a release |
| A run | Lasts an hour at most, and its token dies with it | Long enough for a task's slice, short enough that a token taken from a run soon stops working | The platform owner | The deployment (`A5S_RUN_MINUTES`) |
| Operators' figures by instance | An instance's own go out only if it serves 5 people or more; smaller instances' go out together, once together they serve as many | Fewer, and a figure could say what one person did | The data protection officer | The signed settings (`data_protection.fewest_people`) |
| Objectives | Nine in ten replies within 120 seconds, and 95% of chases before they fall due | What the status measures a5s by, until the first users' baseline sets them | The platform owner | The code, by a release |
| Offers nobody confirmed | A note or a reminder an agent offered lapses after a day unless they say yes | Nothing about someone is kept on an agent's word alone | The data protection officer | The code, by a release |
| A conversation's topic | It stays on its topic for 4 hours without a word | Long enough for a working session, short enough that tomorrow's question starts afresh | The agent owner | The code, by a release |
| Measures of reminders | A reminder for a particular time is on time within an hour of it | A reminder sent in the hour it was asked for is one people count as on time | The platform owner | The code, by a release |

## Phase 2

| Setting | Starting value | Why | Owned by | Where it's set |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Moving up a level | An anytime-valid sequential test showing, with 95% confidence, that accuracy meets the part's threshold, on the grades the core sealed since the cap last changed, pooled across the instances of a behaviour: an agent, its model and the tools of that type it may use, as pinned. With no errors, a 98% threshold needs 224 graded items, and a 95% one 88; with one error, 340 and 134. A critical error, or a fall, means earning it again on new grades. | 50 items can't tell a 98% agent from a 95% one, and a test checked after every grade must stay valid however often it's checked | Each part owner, through the threshold | The signed settings (`thresholds`), and the code for the test |
| Moving down a level | When the grades given since the cap rose show accuracy below the threshold, at once on a critical error, or when the part's threshold rises past what the grades that earned it show. Anyone who may can lower a cap at once (`a5s caps lower`) | A fresh test after each change notices a fall that the grades which earned the level would hide | Each part owner | The signed settings (`thresholds`), and the code |
| Ongoing samples | Changes made at level 2, at random, about 100 a quarter for each behaviour, part and type of action, spread through it, and every held change someone decided while the cap is 1. A sample nobody grades in a quarter lapses | Notices a fall from 98% to 93% about half the time within a quarter's 100, and four times in five within two quarters, with false alarms under one in twenty. To notice it within a quarter four times in five, sample about 200 | Each part owner | The code, by a release |
| Counting backstop | For each type of action, a limit for one run and one for each person acted for in 30 days, as the scenario's settings set them: the sample's are 5 and 20. Past one, a change waits for a person. | Catches a change spread over weeks without stopping a busy assistant | Each part owner | The signed settings (`limits`) |
| Held proposals | Each decider reminded once after 2 working days, in their own hours; once reminded, their managers, or the instance's owner, told after 5; lapsed after 10. A decision by someone whose authenticator was reset in the last day waits until a day has passed. Kept a year after they end. | Nothing waits forever, and a reset, how a stolen identity usually starts, can't approve a change at once | Each part owner | The code, by a release |
| Rubber-stamping flag | Off, until the data protection officer agrees, since it profiles people (O6). Then a decider with at least 3 approvals graded wrong, at over twice the rate of everyone else's, is shown in the status (`data_protection.flag_rubber_stamps`, which the officer approves) | Quick reviews of good work aren't a problem | Assurance | The signed settings (`data_protection.flag_rubber_stamps`), and the code for whom it flags |
| Whom the studio names | Off, until the data protection officer agrees: people's studio pages show for how many people a run was, and not whom (`data_protection.studio_names`, which the officer approves). The core's API, for the team that runs a5s, names them | Who asked an agent what, and when, is about a person even without their words | Assurance | The signed settings (`data_protection.studio_names`) |
| Whom the auditors' page names | Off, until the data protection officer agrees: whom agents acted for shows as a stand-in, the same one each time, and the page exports nothing (`data_protection.audit_names`, which the officer approves). Whoever acted in one of a5s's roles shows as themselves either way | An audit checks what administrators did, and needs patterns, not names, for everyone else | The data protection officer | The signed settings (`data_protection.audit_names`) |
| a5s's reader | The first 24,000 characters of each document, at most 10 documents a call, within what's left of the run's budget, and each document read once a run | One long email can't spend a run's budget, or outlast the agent's wait for its tool | The agent owner | The code, by a release |
| Outside senders | 20 requests a day per outside sender, then the rest wait for review | Stops a flood without blocking a busy supplier | Each part owner | The code, by a release |
| After an authenticator reset | The person's decisions wait 24 hours before they take effect, by the time the directory says it was reset | A reset is how a stolen identity usually starts | The release approver | The code, by a release |

## Phase 3

| Setting | Starting value | Why | Owned by | Where it's set |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Group chats | Up to 20 people, each known to the directory | A run in a group sees only what all of them may see, so a larger group sees little, and each of them counts toward its limits | The platform owner | The code, by a release |
| An answer from another agent | Waited for 2 minutes at most, after which the question is given up | Long enough for a run of a few tool calls, short enough that the person asking isn't left waiting | The platform owner | The code, by a release |
| Notebook entries | The newest 50 for each instance, of 300 characters, each naming up to 10 records. Each goes once its records change, checked each day | A note is worth keeping only while the records it comes from say what it does | The agent owner | The code, by a release |
| Questions one run asks | 3 | Enough to ask two agents and follow one up, and no more of their budgets | The agent owner | The code, by a release |

## Phase 4 and the studio

| Setting | Starting value | Why | Owned by | Where it's set |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A task's slices that fail | A task fails after 5 slices in a row fail or lose their lease, waiting 30 seconds after the first, and twice as long after each; a slice whose run can't open waits 30 minutes | A brief outage costs a task nothing, and a broken one stops | The platform owner | The code, by a release |
| A task's parts | 5,000 tokens at least for each part a task's run splits it into | A part with less can't make a model call worth its cost | The agent owner | The code, by a release |
| Repeating tasks | 5 at once for each person with an agent, offered or going | Enough for a week's routine, few enough that one person's can't fill an agent's time | The platform owner | The code, by a release |
| Reports | 20 facts in one report, and the rest counted | One message stays readable | The agent owner | The code, by a release |
| Drafts | 20 at once for each person in the studio | Enough to work on, few enough to keep tidy | The platform owner | The code, by a release |
| Asking for an agent | An ask nobody answers lapses after 30 days, and one person has one open for each agent | Long enough for its approvers to get to it, short enough that their home doesn't fill with what nobody wants any more | The platform owner | The code, by a release |
