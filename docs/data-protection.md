# Data Protection

**Status:** Draft, for the data protection officer · **Under:** [Architecture](architecture.md)

What the data protection officer's assessment needs to know about a5s before any of the pilot team's data reaches a model ([pilot](pilot.md), stage 2). It says what a5s keeps about people, why, where, for how long and who can see it, what models see, how a request for someone's data is answered, and what the officer decides. The design is in the architecture's [data protection](architecture.md#data-protection); this page is the inventory, from the code.

## Whose data

- **The team's people.** The directory names each of them: their id, such as the identity provider's object id or user name, their name, roles, reporting line, working hours and time zone, and what they may see. a5s reads the directory through the scenario, and its own records name people by their id, though what a5s and the agents say to them uses their names.
- **What they and the agents say to each other,** one to one, and in the group chats they bring an agent into.
- **What the records system holds,** such as customers' questions, names and amounts. It stays in the records system: a run reads it through the scenario's tools, and a5s keeps what the table below says, such as a record's key and title for what someone owes.
- **Whoever decides, grades or approves,** by their id, with what they decided.

## Where it is

- **a5s's database,** a managed Postgres in the deployment's region [TBC: which], and nowhere else. Nothing is copied out, apart from the ledger.
- **The ledger's copies,** in locked object storage in the same region, which the copier writes and nobody can delete before their time.
- **Backups of the database,** as the deployment's Postgres keeps them [TBC: for how long]. One taken before an erasure still holds what went; restoring from it, `a5s restore` erases again each person an erasure since had erased, and names any it can't for the officer to ask again ([runbook](runbooks.md#restoring-from-a-backup)).
- **The models,** each in the region its approved entry names ([`config/models.yaml`](../config/models.yaml)). The model gateway refuses any model not on the list, and a failover to one. A run's calls go to its instance's model, its release's own unless the settings name another, and, while that one isn't answering, to the next its release names as a fallback that may see the same data ([model fallbacks](model-fallbacks.md)).
- **Traces, operators' figures and logs,** in the platform's telemetry, which the deployment names [TBC: which, and where].
- **GitHub,** for a draft its author publishes there, as themselves ([publishing](studio.md#publishing)): its folder, and their name and id in its pull request's description. a5s keeps the pull request's address, in the draft and the ledger, and nothing of their GitHub account: not even the token GitHub gives it for that one publish.
- **The sandbox's database,** a Postgres server of its own, in the same region [TBC], with the scenario's made-up records and what authors said to their drafts there, for an hour after it's quiet. It holds nothing of a5s's database but the drafts tried there, with the test cases kept from runs in them: in its database for an hour after it's quiet, and on its disk until that draft is tried again or the sandbox restarts, which erasure doesn't reach. a5s's holds nothing of it, but who tried which draft, as whom and on which model.

## What models see

- **A run's context:** the conversation it continues, what the records it read hold, its notes, and its instructions. Only what everyone it acts for may see.
- **Not classified values.** The settings classify fields, such as a customer's name as personal, and a model sees only classes its approved entry allows. Others it sees as tokens. Values the agent shouldn't retype, such as contacts and bank accounts, it sees only as handles.
- **Not outside content,** such as an email's body, which only the extractor reads, filling a fixed form.
- **Cached for minutes.** A provider may keep what a run sent for a few minutes, so its next call reads it from a cache, for less. Deep Agents asks Anthropic models to, at the points it marks, and the model gateway asks for the shortest time a provider keeps it, whatever the worker asked. DeepSeek does it of itself. [TBC: the approved provider's terms for what it caches.]
- **For the pilot,** one model on the organisation's approved list for the class of the team's data, such as one it runs in its own subscription. Every model approved as a5s ships, such as DeepSeek's and Qwen's, is hosted by its provider and sees synthetic data only. [TBC: the approved model's provider keeps no prompt, and trains on none, under its terms.]

## Who can see it

- **Each person,** signed in: their own conversations, summaries and inbox, and in a group chat what everyone there heard.
- **An agent's run:** only what everyone it acts for may see, as the directory says.
- **Deciders:** the changes they may decide, on the core's page, signed in afresh for each decision.
- **Graders:** samples of records they may see, on the core's page. Never anyone who owns, authors or drafted the agent, nor anyone from their teams, nor anyone a change was for or who decided it.
- **An instance's owner:** its notes whose records they may see, on the core's page.
- **Everyone signed in:** in the studio's catalogue, the agents whose owners list them for everyone, each by name, what it does and who owns it, and the agents that serve them, with what each may do with their records. Only those with a role on an agent nobody lists know of it, as a name such as redundancy planning says too much on its own.
- **The people who run an instance, own its agent, own or decide for one of its parts, or approve instances:** on the studio's pages, under `/studio/`, its figures, which count people and never name them, and the runs whose every record they may see: when each ran, what kind of thing started it, for how many people, the keys of the records it handled, which a5s keeps with the run, and its tool calls with the policy's decisions and their ids. Nothing anyone said, not even by its hash, which a short text can be guessed back from. Whom each run acted for, by name, only once the officer agrees, as deciders and graders see whom a change was for: until then it's off (`data_protection.studio_names` in the signed settings, which they approve). [TBC: the officer, before the pilot's stage 1.]
- **The team that runs a5s:** the status and the measures, which count people and never name them; the held changes that wait, by whom they're for (`a5s proposals`); the ledger, by ids and hashes; and every instance's runs, with whom each acted for and its entries in the ledger (`a5s runs`).
- **Auditors and assurance:** the ledger, by ids and hashes, for someone the directory names as an auditor or assurance: a period's entries, found as they ask, and a report of who holds each role and who did what. On their own page, in the studio, `/studio/audit`, signed in, with a second factor where the deployment asks for one, whoever acted in one of a5s's roles, approving, deciding, signing or reading the trail, shows as themselves. Everyone else an entry names, whom agents acted for, told or asked about, such as the subject of the officer's request, shows as a stand-in, the same one each time, which a key only the database holds makes, and so does someone who has left, whom the directory no longer names, until their archive goes. A stand-in is a pseudonym, not anonymity: someone who knows when a person used an agent could tell which stand-in is theirs. An id finds nothing there, so the page never says whether agents acted for someone it names. Names, and exports of a period, are on the page only once the officer agrees (`data_protection.audit_names` in the signed settings, which they approve), since an export holds the ledger exactly, ids and all. Until then the team that runs a5s exports a period for them with `a5s audit export`, and an export that someone changed doesn't match the ledger's locked copies, which the team can't change. Each read, on the page or through the team, is itself in the ledger, with whom they asked about as a stand-in, never their id or a hash of it, which anyone with the directory could work back, and through what it came: their own sign-in, or, only where nobody signs in, as in development, the service token naming them ([administration](administration.md)). [TBC: the officer, before auditors use the page.]
- **The administrators' page,** in the studio, `/studio/admin`, for anyone with one of a5s's roles, or from the team that runs it: who holds each role, by name, and whether the directory or a group gives it; how many people's sign-ins showed each group; what waits for whom, with held changes by part and who decides them, never what they change; and the latest administrative acts, by whom, with whom each was about as a stand-in until the officer agrees, as on the auditors' page. Each look is in the ledger.
- **Anyone with access to the database itself** sees everything a5s keeps. [TBC: who, with the cloud's access reviews, and its audit log of who connected.]
- **The data protection officer:** the record of whose archive is which, and for a request to see someone's data, all a5s keeps about them, which someone from the team that runs a5s carries out for them.

## A request for someone's data

- **How the officer asks.** In the studio, `/studio/officer`, signed in afresh for that request alone: to export, or erase, all a5s keeps about one person. Someone from the team that runs a5s carries it out, once, within ten minutes, with the core's service token: `a5s export --person <id> --out <file>`, or `a5s erase --person <id> --confirm <id>`. Neither can do it alone: the team can't without the officer's request, and the officer's sign-in does nothing else, for nobody else, and only once. The ledger records the request, with the sign-in by its hash, and what was done ([`core/officer.py`](../src/a5s/core/officer.py)).
- **To see it:** all a5s keeps that names them, as the table above lists it, one holder at a time. Since the directory may give a leaver's id to someone new, an export by id gives what's its holder's since its last archive, and lists its archives, each of which may be someone else's who held the id before: the officer exports each that's the person's by its own name. It gives nobody else's words: in a group chat, what they said, and their corrections to its summary, and how much they heard others say or were answered there, and of a question agents asked for others too, only that it was. It names records by key and title, and changes by what they do, never what records held, which is the records system's to answer for, nor whom else a change was for. What the ledger and runs hold, which is what was said only by hash, it counts, by what each entry records. The ledger records each export, by whom and for whom, in counts ([`core/export.py`](../src/a5s/core/export.py)). It gives the tasks they asked agents to do, in their words, with what each did, and those they asked to be done again and again, and counts those someone else asked for them.
- **Someone outside the directory,** such as a customer who writes to a shared mailbox, the officer names by their address, as for anyone else. a5s keeps little of them, and briefly: whom a message came from reaches a run as a handle, whose value goes an hour after the run, with what a5s's reader read of their mail. Their address can stay longer where a5s wrote it out for someone, such as in an answer to the team, where someone wrote it in a message or a task, and in the words of a change an agent asked for. An export says where a5s keeps it, and how much, from when to when, with the changes that name them; the texts themselves are other people's conversations, which the officer reads as the organisation's records if the request needs them. An erasure puts a stand-in for their address in each of those texts, lets go of each handle and document of theirs, and keeps what's evidence of what agents did, the changes asked for that name them and their samples, for as long as retention keeps them, as for anyone. While a run holds their address, or a change that names them waits for a decision, it refuses, saying which, and the team tries again once that's over. The ledger keeps their address by a digest alone ([`core/customers.py`](../src/a5s/core/customers.py)).
- **To forget a note** on how they like to work: they ask an agent, and it goes at once, leaving no words in the ledger.
- **To correct a summary:** they ask the agent, which corrects it, as they see it.
- **To erase it:** as for an export, one holder at a time. By an id, a5s erases what's its holder's since its last archive: their conversations and summaries, notes, preferences, drafts of agents and reminders, what reports told them, their days' budget and digests, what they said in group chats, and each group's summary that held any of it, which the group's next run makes again from what's left, the questions agents asked for them alone, what they said they were waiting on, what they owed that the records no longer owe, what they asked of runs whose changes were drawn as samples, and the test cases kept from runs that hold what they said, from every draft that holds them; where code can't take one out, such as from a file that isn't YAML, it says so, keeping whose it is, for the officer to have it put right and erase it again. While they're still here, it keeps where a5s writes to them, without a word they said, so what's for them, such as a decision's card, still reaches them. An archive of the id may be someone else's, so it stays, and is listed: the officer erases each that's the person's by its name, which erases it with the record of whose it was. It keeps what's evidence of what agents did, for as long as retention keeps it: the ledger, which keeps what was said only by hash, runs, which name whom they acted for, the changes asked for them with their decisions and samples, and what they owe while the records owe it. Where they were in a group chat, a name no person has stands in their place, and only the runs kept as evidence link it back to them; the agents' answers there are the group's and stay, with what others said was wrong with them. While a run is at work for them, which might write what it read of them after they're gone, it refuses, and the team tries again in a minute or two. The id, sent twice, guards against a mistyped one, and the ledger records each erasure, by whom and for whom, in counts ([`core/erasure.py`](../src/a5s/core/erasure.py)). It erases the tasks they asked agents to do, with what each did, and those they asked to be done again and again, and refuses, as for a run, while a task's slice is at work for them.
- **What the records system holds** is the records system's to answer for.

## Measures

`a5s measures` and the status count people and never name them. Measures of individual people judge outcomes, not speed, and they're agreed with the officer, and with works councils where there are any, before they start ([assurance](architecture.md#assurance)). The flag for deciders who approve what graders find wrong profiles people, so it's off until the officer agrees ([starting values](starting-values.md)).

## What the officer decides

Seven of the officer's choices take effect in a5s itself, in the signed settings' `data_protection`, so they need no release, and nobody else can make them. A version of the settings that changes any of them needs the approval of the officer the directory names, besides whoever else it needs. The officer approves only that, and a group being given the officer's own role, which lets its members export and erase what a5s keeps about anyone: their approval counts towards nothing else a version decides. Until they choose otherwise, each is a5s's own, which keeps people's data closest:

| Choice | a5s's own | What it decides |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `kept_for` | 12 months | How long a5s keeps what's said, after a conversation's last message, and what goes with it: summaries, notes, reminders, what people owed, held changes and their decisions, samples, questions agents asked each other, and tasks. From a day to ten years, in days or months |
| `fewest_people` | 5 | The fewest people an instance serves before its own figures go out to the team's dashboards. Fewer, and they go out with other small instances', once together they serve as many. 3 at least |
| `studio_names` | false | Whether the studio's pages name whom each run acted for, rather than for how many. Until they do, an instance serving fewer than `fewest_people` shows no run one by one, only its daily figures, since each run would say what one of a few people did; and the studio never shows whom an instance serves, only how many |
| `audit_names` | false | Whether the auditors' page names whom agents acted for, and exports the ledger there |
| `flag_rubber_stamps` | false | Whether the status names deciders whose approvals are graded wrong far more often than others' (O6) |
| `cases_from_runs` | false | Whether those who author an agent may keep one of its runs as a test case: what someone said to it, with the names of whom it acted for and everyone a5s serves, emails, links, numbers of six digits or more, dates of birth, ids such as a passport's or an IBAN, street addresses and postcodes, and the records' keys masked by code, which its author reads, and confirms says nothing about anyone, before keeping it in their draft, and in the agent's folder once it's published. Only a run they may see, every record its words name too, of an instance whose runs show one by one ([`core/run_cases.py`](../src/a5s/core/run_cases.py)) |
| `shadow_runs` | false | Whether a new version of an agent may answer what people ask an instance, beside it, as a shadow, sending and changing nothing, for graders to compare its answers with those sent before anyone puts it to work. A shadow reads what people say and what their records hold, as the instance it shadows does, maybe on a model approved for each class of what that instance's model may read, and graders read what was asked and both answers, only of runs whose every record they may see and every document they may read: until the officer agrees to names, with the names of everyone a5s serves and who runs its instances, emails, links and long numbers masked by code, though someone else the directory knows, named in passing, stays as written, and none of an instance serving fewer people than they chose ([agent model](agent-model.md#shadow-runs), [`core/shadows.py`](../src/a5s/core/shadows.py)) |

Whoever approves a version reads each of these it changes, in words, as its approval card, the approving page and the settings' review in the studio say it, and each that names people, keeps what's said longer, lets an instance's figures out when it serves fewer people, keeps runs as test cases or lets shadows answer is marked as widening what a5s does. The whole version, as each approver reviews it before approving it, says all seven, and what a version still needs names the officer from the start. Each choice takes effect once the version is in force, on every page: the channels app, which serves the pages and holds no key to check what was approved on them, reads the choices of the version the core checked in force and sealed.

For example, a version keeping things for two years, with the studio naming people:

```yaml
data_protection:
  kept_for: 24 months
  studio_names: true
```

They were the deployment's to set once, as `A5S_STUDIO_NAMES`, `A5S_AUDIT_NAMES` and `A5S_FLAG_RUBBER_STAMPS`, which the team that runs a5s could set alone. A part now refuses to start with any of them set, and says where it went.
