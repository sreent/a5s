# Phase 2

**Status:** Built, 31 October 2026, apart from a limit for each outside sender ([progress](#progress)) · Round 6 of the critique has read it, and what it found is fixed · **Under:** [Architecture](architecture.md)

Phase 2 lets agents act. In [phase 1](phase-1.md) they read, report, draft and remind, and change no data. In phase 2 an agent can propose a change, which the owner of what it touches decides on the core's own page. Once samples, graded by people outside the team that built it, show it's accurate enough, it can make changes of a type the part owner approves, within the scenario's limits, telling someone each time. Content from outside, such as a customer's email, reaches agents only through the extractor, and a run that has read some never acts on its own.

## Decide first

Confirmed on 1 November 2026:

| Decision | Proposed |
| --- | --- |
| What agents can change in the sample | The assistant can change a customer question's due date, and close one, in the sample's records system. Each is a type of action: `change-due-date` and `close-question`. Both are frequent enough for the assistant to come to act on its own, can be undone, and move no money. What sends outside or can't be undone stays with a person, decided one at a time. A deployment's first types of action are the same kind: frequent, undoable changes inside the organisation, such as a date, a status or who a case is assigned to. |
| Who decides a held change | The owners of each part its records are in, and anyone they name to decide beside them (`part-delegate:<part>`), with an approval for each part, never anyone the run acts for. Where nobody else may decide, a5s doesn't hold the change: the agent drafts it for the person to make themselves. |
| Which decisions take a sign-in each | Decisions on sensitive changes: a tool that can't be undone or sends outside the organisation, or a value of a class of its own. Others can be decided together, with one sign-in that names each of them. |
| How long a held change waits | Each decider is reminded once after 2 working days, in their own hours; once reminded, their managers, or else the instance's owner or deputy, never a decider nor whom it's for, are told after 5, and whoever asked hears it waits still; it lapses after 10, told to whoever asked and to those it waited for ([starting values](starting-values.md#phase-2)) |
| Who grades | People who hold the `grader` role, never from the team of the agent's owner or of the instance's owner or deputy, nor reporting to whoever decided the change, and only for records they may see. In each deployment, two or three for each part, from a quality or controls team close to the work, such as finance controls for billing, with a one-page guide to right, wrong and critical, and at least two for each part, so nobody's leave stops the grading. Coming to act on its own at 98% takes about 224 grades, some 11 hours, from at least two graders, since no grader's grades count toward a raise beyond everyone else's together; after that, 100 to 200 a quarter. One sample in five is drawn twice, for a second grader, and counts as the worse of its two grades (round 15, C4). |
| Thresholds | Each part's, in its settings. The sample: billing 98%, orders 95%. In a deployment: 98% for anything that touches money or what customers are told, 95% for the organisation's own housekeeping, and never lower. |
| The sequential test | Anytime-valid, at 95% confidence, so checking after every grade doesn't make a raised cap likelier by chance. With no errors, a 98% threshold needs 224 graded items and a 95% one 88, more than the 149 and 59 a test of fixed size needs, which the starting values quoted until now. |
| Outside content in the sample | Made-up emails from each record's customer |

## Build, in order

Each step ends with something running and its tests passing.

1. **Held proposals and signed decisions.**
   - A scenario's tool says what type of action it takes, if it changes a part's data, such as `change-due-date`, and names the record it changes. The type is pinned in the release with the rest of the tool.
   - When the agent asks first, policy holds such a call rather than allowing it: a hold is an annotation on a Cedar permit. The core keeps the exact call, the records it names as they stood, and the values of any handles in it, and tells the agent who decides. The agent carries on.
   - Its deciders get a card, or a link in other chats, that opens the core's page. There they see what the change does, in words code works out, with the agent's flags, such as values it wrote itself. They sign in afresh, with a nonce that commits to their decision on exactly that change, or on a list of changes that aren't sensitive, and to the words of a rejection's reason, as they wrote it. The core seals the decision, with its reason made fit to tell whoever asked, and each sign-in decides once.
   - Once it's approved, the gateway makes the call, if the records haven't changed and the instance may still act. Whoever the run acted for is told what was decided, and so is the agent's next run, as a5s's notice.
   - Its deciders are reminded once after two working days, their managers are told after five, and it lapses after ten. A decider whose authenticator was reset in the last day has their decisions wait until a day has passed.
2. **Counts.** The settings give the scenario's limits for each type of action: how many one piece of work may take, and how many for each person acted for in 30 days. Code decides what a piece of work is: a run. The gateway counts in the transaction that decides each call, under a lock, and a call past a limit is held for its decider, whatever the agent may do.
3. **Types of action and acting on its own.**
   - The settings set what an agent may do for each part and type of action. A setting to act on its own counts only while one of the people who approved it for that part still owns the part, and drops to asking first otherwise, until the new owner approves a version.
   - When the agent acts on its own, a call runs, and is reported: in the digest of the instance's owner, with a link to the record, and to whoever the run acted for. The owner reverses one through the scenario's own process.
   - A run that has read outside content asks first at most, so what it would change is held.
4. **Samples, grades and the cap.**
   - a5s keeps a cap for each behaviour, part and type of action, and an instance does only what both its setting and the cap allow. A behaviour is the agent, its model and the tools of that type it may use, as pinned, so each model a release may run on, its own, one its instance's settings choose, or one it falls back to, earns its own cap, and a change is made on its own only where every model that answered its run earned it (round 16). To begin with, the cap holds it to asking first.
   - Code draws samples: every held change once it's decided, while the cap holds it to asking first, and enough of the actions it takes on its own for about 100 a quarter. Graders grade them on the core's page as right, wrong or critical, seeing what the agent proposed or did and the record as it stood.
   - A sequential test, pooled across the behaviour's instances, on the grades given since the cap last changed, raises the cap when the evidence shows accuracy meets the part's threshold, and lowers it when the evidence shows it doesn't, or at once on a critical error. The ledger records each change of cap.
   - The status flags a decider whose approvals are graded wrong more often than their peers'. A release that widens what an agent does starts by asking first for what it widens.
5. **The extractor and marked runs.**
   - A scenario's tool says which fields of what it returns are outside content, and the fixed schema the extractor fills from them: keys, amounts, dates, choices from a list, and sensitive values, never free text.
   - The gateway never hands those fields to the agent. The extractor, a model call with no tools and the instance's approved model, fills the schema, and code keeps only values it finds in the text itself, giving sensitive ones as handles.
   - An agent can also ask a question about a document. The answer comes back in free text, marked as outside content, and never as an instruction.
   - The run is marked from then: it can't act on its own or write anyone's profile, and what it drafts or proposes says it drew on outside content.

Outside senders who can start work were to have a limit of 20 requests a day each. Mail to a scenario's shared mailboxes now starts work, and that limit isn't built yet ([progress](#progress)).
