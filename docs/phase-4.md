# Phase 4: resumable long-running tasks

**Status:** The engine is built, and tasks run on it: people ask an agent for one, say yes, and a5s works on it in runs of its own, in parts if its runs split it. People see and stop theirs on a page of their own, and ask for one to be done again and again · **Under:** [Architecture](architecture.md)

a5s already keeps work in records rather than in a process that must stay alive. Long-running tasks extend that rule: a task is a durable record, and each piece of work runs for a bounded time on a fresh worker when needed. There is no permanent LangGraph thread and no hidden state in a worker.

## Progress

- [`core/tasks.py`](../src/a5s/core/tasks.py) and migration `0042_tasks.sql` implement the durable record, checkpoints, heartbeat-backed leases, stale-worker recovery, fresh-worker resume, cancellation, token/deadline limits, blocked/resume handling, and constrained parent/child tasks, with ledger evidence. `tests/test_tasks.py` exercises them against real Postgres.
- Migration `0043_task_plans.sql` adds each task's plan, which checkpoints and results set and the core checks: at most 20 steps of 200 characters, one in progress at a time, and what's completed kept. The ledger keeps the plan by hash, with how many steps it has and how many are completed.
- Round 9 of the critique ([critique](critique.md)) made the budget and the leases hold. A task and the children it makes are a tree, which shares its root's budget: each task's own budget is the most it may spend, and the tree together spends no more than its root's. Every token a slice reports counts, past a budget too, and a report past one ends the task at once, so nothing it spends goes uncounted; each slice is told what's left, `tokens_left`, for the run it opens to be held to once the scheduler opens runs for tasks. A slice that fails, or loses its lease, leaves its task waiting 30 seconds, then twice as long each time, to an hour, and after 5 in a row the task fails. A result the core refuses, such as a plan that rewords a completed step, blocks the task for a person to decide, rather than leave it to be done again. A heartbeat that fails is tried again at the next beat, so a database's moment doesn't lapse a live lease, and a worker whose lease was taken over can't end the new one's. Cancelling a task cancels its children, and what the slice at work did meanwhile is kept. Only the core writes a task: the scheduler reads them. Tasks are in the export, erasure, archiving and retention ([data protection](data-protection.md)).
- [`core/tasking.py`](../src/a5s/core/tasking.py) and migration `0046_task_runs.sql` run tasks as a5s's own work. An agent whose release names `tasks` offers one in the person's own chat (`offer_task`), and it starts only once they say yes in a later message (`confirm_task`), as a reminder does, with the release's budget and days. People name a task by its number, such as T-12, and list and stop theirs through the agent (`list_tasks`, `cancel_task`). The scheduler asks the core which tasks are due (`/tasks/sweep`) and works on each as it does a schedule, under a lease of the task's own, so a task at work never holds up its owner's chat. Each slice is a run of the agent's, acting for the task's audience, on its owner's day: it's told the task, its plan, what its earlier runs found and the most it may spend, and the model gateway holds each of its calls to that. It keeps its work with `checkpoint_task` and says what it came to with `finish_task`: done, blocked on its owner, or waiting to go on. A slice whose run can't open, such as when its owner's budget for the day is spent, puts the task off half an hour rather than leave it to be asked for at every sweep, and past its deadline the task fails. The slice's lease lasts as long as its run may, and it ends as the run closes, with the run's own count of its tokens, whatever the worker says, so a run that dies still counts, once the runs' sweep closes it. Only once it's done or needs them may the run write to its owner, and to nobody else; when the task ends without a word from its run, such as when its deadline passes or its budget runs out, a5s tells them itself, in its own words, and only then. Each slice is given what the task's earlier runs read, with where each value came from, and nothing else, and opens marked if any of them, or the run that offered it, read content from outside (`task_reads`, `tasks.read_outside`): so a value an earlier run made up is still its agent's own when a later one writes it, and the mark follows what they drew on. What its agent wrote of the task, in `offer_task` and `list_tasks`, never counts as read. When a slice says the task is blocked on its owner, they say what it needs in their own chat, and the agent goes on with it with `resume_task`: its next run is told what it needed and what they said. A handle is good only in the run it was given to, so a slice looks a value up again rather than keep its handle. `tests/test_task_runs.py` runs it end to end through the scheduler.
- People see their own tasks on a page of their own in the channels app, `/tasks/`, signed in as on the notebook's: each task they asked any agent for, how it stands, its plan, why it stopped or what it needed and what they said, its deadline and what it has spent, and a button to cancel it, which the ledger records as theirs. A task whose words name a record they may no longer see shows how it stands alone. The channels app reads tasks as its own role, and may change only a task's status to cancel it (migration `0047_task_page.sql`).
- A task's run can split its work into parts that can be done apart (`split_task`), up to 8, each a task of its own that a5s works on in runs of its own, at once if its pool has room. Each part gets an equal share of what the task has left, keeping one share for the task's own later runs, and the task's deadline. The task waits while its parts are at work, and goes on once they've all ended, told what each came to. A part writes to nobody and can't wait on its owner: the task it's part of speaks for it, and only the task's own end reaches its owner, from its run or from a5s. A run of any task in the tree is given what every run of it read, with where each value came from, and opens marked if any of them read content from outside. Their owner sees, through the agent and on their page, how many of a task's parts are done.
- People ask an agent for a task to be done again and again, such as every Monday at 09:00, in their own chat: the agent offers it with `offer_task`, saying its days and time, and it starts once they say yes in a later message, as a task does ([`core/repeats.py`](../src/a5s/core/repeats.py), migration `0048_task_repeats.sql`). At each of its times, in its owner's time zone, the tasks' sweep makes a task of it, with the release's budget and days for one, and what the run that offered it had read, with where each value came from, through the existing schedule machinery. It makes one only once the last task it made has ended, so they never pile up, and a time that comes round meanwhile is let go, which the ledger records. A person has at most 5 with an agent at once, names one by its number, such as E-3, and lists and stops it through the agent or on their own page. Stopping it leaves its last task to go on. Archiving its owner stops it; erasure, export and retention cover it.

## Done means

A person can start a task that needs more than one run, watch its state and partial results, stop it, and resume it after a failure without repeating effects. The task has an owner, an instance, an audience, a total budget, a deadline, and evidence in the ledger. If it blocks, a person decides what happens next. No task can widen its own permissions, budget, audience, or deadline.

## Build in this order

1. **The task record.** A durable id, owner, instance, audience, task text, status, current step, partial results, total token/duration budget, deadline, and optional parent. States are queued, running, waiting, blocked, done, cancelled and failed. The core alone writes its state through its own interfaces.
2. **Bounded runs and checkpoints.** Each run opens for the task's current step. Code records progress and partial results at tool/action boundaries, never only in the model's context. A new run reads the task record, not an old worker's memory. The record keeps the task's plan too: up to 20 steps, each with its content and whether it's pending, in progress or completed, in the shape of LangChain's todos, which models know from `write_todos`. A run sets it at its checkpoints, the next run is shown it, and the owner sees it. A step once completed stays completed, in its words, so the plan moves forward as the task does. Deep Agents' own `write_todos` stays off: its list would live in one run's state, and go with it.
3. **Leases and heartbeats.** An active worker renews the task's lease while it works. A stalled lease expires, but one with a live heartbeat does not. A lease is not a budget: both have limits.
4. **Resume and cancellation.** A fresh worker resumes after failure or expiry, with no repeated idempotent effect. Cancellation takes effect at the next core call and stops future runs. A user can see what was already done.
5. **Budgets and blocked work.** The core counts all runs against the task's total budget and deadline. When blocked, the task waits with a reason and its owner or another authorized person decides whether and how to resume it.
6. **Parent and child tasks.** A parent may create only children allowed by its release, for the same or a narrower audience, with the same provenance marks, shared budget and a bounded depth/count. The ledger links each child to its parent. This is not the unrestricted Deep Agents `task` tool.
7. **People's task scheduling.** Where a person asks for a recurring task, it uses the same durable record, an explicit proposal and confirmation, and the existing scheduler. It changes or cancels only with that person's request. This is an extension of the existing reminder/schedule machinery, not another scheduler.

## Tests

- A task survives a worker, scheduler and core restart and resumes without repeating an effect.
- A worker that stops heartbeating loses its lease; one that keeps working does not.
- Cancellation, budget exhaustion and deadlines stop future work and tell the owner what was done.
- A child cannot exceed its parent's audience, permissions, marks, budget, depth or count.
- A user-created schedule needs confirmation, can be changed or cancelled, and acts only for its owner.
- Partial results and every state change are in the ledger, with text kept by hash under the existing data-protection rules.
- A soak test runs tasks through restarts and finds no duplicate effects, lost tasks or growing leases.

## What this does not change

No permanent agent process, unrestricted sub-agent tool, hidden worker state, self-widening permissions, or autonomous approval of blocked work. The existing service-desk and other scenario PRs stay independent of this phase until they need long-running tasks.
